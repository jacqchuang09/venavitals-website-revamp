The Problem

The current choice is difficult.

⏱

A-line delay

Placing an arterial line can take 5 to 40 minutes, requires a skilled operator, and introduces procedural risk before the first incision. For cases that fall in the middle, where continuous pressure would be useful but a line is not clearly indicated, the choice between monitoring gaps and invasive access is a real clinical trade-off.

⚠

A-line risk

Arterial lines carry a 10 to 13% complication rate and a 0.6% infection risk per placement. In high-volume OR environments, that adds up across a year of cases.

◔

Cuff blind spots

A cuff reads every 3 to 5 minutes with a 30 to 60 second lag. Sudden blood loss, fluid shifts, and rapid pressure changes can happen and resolve, or worsen, inside that window. The anesthesiologist sees the drop at the next reading, not when it starts.

How VeriTrack Fits

Apply in pre-op, on the foot, out of the field.

VeriTrack is placed over the dorsalis pedis artery on the foot before the patient enters the OR. It streams beat-to-beat blood pressure to a bedside iOS device throughout the case. No arm access. No sterile field disruption. No interval re-application. After emergence, it peels off cleanly: no arterial line removal, no site care.

1

Pre-op, under 5 minutes

Sensor applied to the foot. Bluetooth pairs to the iOS app. Signal confirmed before incision.

2

Continuous through the case

Beat-to-beat SBP, DBP, and MAP visible throughout. Every heartbeat, not every 3–5 minutes.

3

Zero disruption to surgical field

Foot placement keeps the sensor entirely away from the operative site and anesthesia workspace.
