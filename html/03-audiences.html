Vena Vitals Website — Target Audiences

Priority order drives every design and content decision. When two audiences conflict, the higher-priority one wins.

Priority 1: Hospital Administrators / Health-System Decision Makers

Who they are: VPs of perioperative services, ICU/OR directors, supply chain & value-analysis committee members, CMIOs, CFO-adjacent buyers. They arrive doing field research before or during a purchase evaluation.

What they're trying to answer in the first 60 seconds:

  • 1. What does this device do, and what does it replace or augment? (arterial lines? cuffs?)
  • 2. Is it FDA-cleared / what's the regulatory status?
  • 3. Is there clinical evidence — and from credible institutions?
  • 4. What does it cost / what's the economic case (ROI, reduced complications, nursing time, consumables)?
  • 5. Who else is using it? (social proof: pilot hospitals, academic partners)
  • 6. How do I talk to a human / get a demo / start an evaluation?

Content they need on the site:

  • Plain-language value proposition (clinical AND economic)
  • Evidence page: validation studies, accuracy data vs. arterial line / cuff, publications
  • Regulatory status stated clearly and honestly
  • Economic case: cost of hypotension/complications, workflow savings — even a 1-pager PDF
  • "Partner with us" / pilot program path, demo request form
  • Credibility: team, advisors, institutional partners, funding, press
  • Downloadable assets they can circulate internally (spec sheet, evidence summary, white paper) — admins sell internally on your behalf

Tone: confident, evidence-first, no consumer-wellness fluff. They are allergic to hype and will discount unverifiable claims.

Priority 2: Anesthesiologists (and intensivists / perioperative clinicians)

Who they are: Clinical end users; often the internal champions who push administration to evaluate. Skeptical, data-literate, time-poor.

What they're trying to answer:

  • 1. How does it work? (sensor principle, where it's placed, waveform quality)
  • 2. How accurate is it vs. the gold standard (A-line)? Show me Bland-Altman, AAMI/ISO 81060 compliance, validation methodology.
  • 3. Does it give beat-to-beat waveform or just intermittent values? Latency?
  • 4. How does it fit my workflow — setup time, interference, monitor integration (Philips/GE/Epic?), alarms?
  • 5. What patient populations / clinical settings has it been tested in (OR, ICU, ED, obesity, arrhythmia, vasopressors)?
  • 6. Who are the clinical investigators behind it?

Content they need:

  • Technology page with real technical depth (don't dumb it down — a layered page: simple top, detailed below)
  • Clinical evidence library: every publication, abstract, poster, conference talk — filterable, with PDFs where possible
  • Specs table: accuracy, sample rate, measurement range, sensor placement, single-use vs reusable
  • Workflow / "in use" content: photos or video of the device on a patient in a real setting
  • Named clinical advisors / investigators (credibility transfers from people)
  • A path to participate: clinical trial interest, early-access program

Tone: technical, precise, citation-backed. Anesthesiologists are the hardest audience to impress and the most valuable champion if convinced.

Priority 3 (explicitly deprioritized): Consumers / Patients

The website is not a consumer product site. Consumer content is limited to:

  • A short FAQ entry or one paragraph ("Is this available for home use?" → status statement)
  • Accessible plain-language explanation on the homepage (which also serves admins)

Design implication: no consumer-wellness aesthetics (no lifestyle-smartwatch imagery, no app-store badges, no "track your health" language). Clinical-grade brand throughout.

Secondary audiences to keep in mind (don't design for, don't block)

  • Investors — will visit; served by News/About/Team pages.
  • Talent — careers page or at minimum a "join us" link.
  • Researchers / potential clinical-trial sites — served by the evidence page + a "collaborate" CTA.
  • Press — newsroom with press kit (logo, boilerplate, leadership photos).
