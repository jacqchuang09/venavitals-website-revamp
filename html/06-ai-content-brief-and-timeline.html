PROJECT BRIEF: Vena Vitals website first draft

Company: Vena Vitals — medical device startup (founded 2019, spun out of UC Irvine). Product: VeriTrack, a soft, flexible, band-aid-like capacitive pressure sensor using patented "wrinkled metal" technology. Placed on the skin over a palpable artery (currently the dorsalis pedis on the foot), it captures continuous, beat-to-beat arterial blood pressure noninvasively — no cuff, no arterial line. Data streams via Bluetooth to a tablet.

Stage: FDA 510(k) submitted, NOT yet cleared. Tested on 600+ patients in operating rooms; tracked rapid BP changes closely against side-by-side arterial lines. Backed by Y Combinator, MedTech Innovator, EvoNexus, federal grants; investors include Samsung NEXT. Founders: Ray Liu (CEO, medtech/digital-health veteran), Prof. Michelle Khine (UCI Biomedical Engineering, inventor), Josh Kim (PhD inventor, lead author of foundational papers).

Audience priority: (1) hospital administrators researching a potential purchase/pilot, (2) anesthesiologists as clinical evaluators and champions, (3) consumers — explicitly NOT served; no consumer-wellness tone, imagery, or app-store framing.

The problem we solve (use this framing): Blood pressure monitoring offers two bad options — cuffs (snapshots every few minutes; hypotensive events missed between cycles) and arterial lines (gold standard but invasive, risky, and take 10–40 unpredictable minutes to place, delaying surgery starts).

Voice: Confident, precise, evidence-first. Write like a clinical company, not a wellness brand. Short declarative sentences. Every claim either cited or visibly conservative. Plain English on top-level pages; real technical depth (sensing mechanism, validation methodology) on Technology and Evidence pages — anesthesiologists must not feel talked down to.

Hard rules:

  • 1. Never say or imply FDA-cleared, approved, or commercially available. Standard line on every product page: "VeriTrack has been submitted for FDA 510(k) review and is not yet available for commercial sale."
  • 2. "Comparable to the arterial line" only alongside the validation study reference.
  • 3. No outcome claims (mortality, AKI reduction, etc.) — we don't have that data.
  • 4. Sleep and home applications = "future directions," not products.
  • 5. Mark every fact you're unsure of with [VERIFY] for human review.
  • 6. Primary CTA everywhere: "Request a Demo." Secondary: "See the Evidence."

Deliverable: Full draft copy for: Home, Technology, Clinical Evidence, Solutions/OR (plus short ICU and Sleep variants), Partner With Us (incl. FAQ), About, Contact, plus footer/disclaimer text. Follow the section order in the wireframes exactly. Write real headlines (punchy, ≤8 words) and real body copy (no lorem ipsum, no placeholders except [VERIFY] flags and [IMAGE: description] slots).

Part 3 — Two-week plan

Week 1 — content and structure

  • Day 1–2: Team reviews this package; finalize sitemap & wireframes; kick off asset checklist; set up Wix Studio site shell (pages, nav, fonts, colors); assign an owner for regulatory copy approval.
  • Day 3–4: Run the AI content brief → first-pass copy for all pages. Human edit pass: fact-check every [VERIFY], tighten headlines.
  • Day 5: Clinical/regulatory review of copy (Khine/Liu + counsel for the disclaimer). Lock the copy deck.

Week 2 — build (in Wix Studio)

  • Day 6–8: Build all pages from wireframes + locked copy; create the CMS collections (Publications, News) and connect repeaters; drop in real assets (placeholder only where assets are still pending); wire up demo form → email notifications + CRM; set up analytics.
  • Day 9: Internal review on desktop + mobile; collect feedback in one doc (not dribbled).
  • Day 10: Apply feedback; QA pass (links, form submission test, load speed, typos); stage on preview URL → first draft delivered.

Post-draft (week 3+): show 2-3 friendly anesthesiologists and one hospital-admin contact, iterate, then cut over DNS.

Part 4 — Aesthetic direction (for the designer/AI builder)

  • Palette: white base, deep navy text, one accent (medical teal or arterial red used sparingly for waveform motifs and CTAs). No gradients-on-gradients, no consumer pastels.
  • Typography: modern grotesque sans (e.g., Inter, Söhne-like) — bold oversized stat numerals, generous whitespace.
  • Imagery: real clinical settings + clean product renders + waveform/data motifs. The continuous-waveform-vs-cuff-snapshot graphic should become a recurring brand element.
  • Motion: restrained — a live-drawing waveform animation in the hero is the one place to spend animation budget.
  • Anti-patterns: stock photos of smiling people pointing at tablets; smartwatch-lifestyle imagery; crypto-startup dark mode.
