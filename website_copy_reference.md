### M3 -- The problem
**Headline:** Blood pressure is continuous. Monitoring is not.

**Body:** The standard cuff reads every 3 to 5 minutes. Between readings, a patient can lose significant blood volume or undergo a dangerous pressure drop that goes undetected until the next cycle. The alternative -- arterial line cannulation -- provides continuous, beat-to-beat data, but carries a 10-13% complication rate, a 0.6% infection risk, and a 5 to 20 minute placement time that delays the start of surgery.

**Stats:**
- Cuff: checks every 3-5 min
- Arterial line: 10-13% complication rate, 0.6% infection risk, 5-20 min placement time

**Pull-quote:** "It's very binary... there's nothing really in between." -- Anesthesiologist (inVibe survey, n=22)

---

### M4 -- The solution
**Headline:** Continuous. Non-invasive. Beat-to-beat.

**Body:** VeriTrack is a soft, wearable sensor that adheres to the foot and measures blood pressure with every heartbeat -- no cuff cycles, no needle. It delivers the continuous hemodynamic visibility of an arterial line without the procedural risk, placement delay, or invasive cannulation.

VeriTrack has been submitted for FDA 510(k) review and is not yet available for commercial sale.

**3 differentiators:**
1. Beat-to-beat resolution -- every heartbeat, not every few minutes
2. Non-invasive placement -- no cannulation, no infection risk
3. Wearable form factor -- soft sensor, foot placement, no disruption to surgical field

### M2 -- How it works
**Plain:** The sensor sits over a palpable artery. Its soft, stretchable material detects the artery's deflections beneath the skin, capturing every heartbeat with high fidelity, sensitivity, and resolution.

**Technical (expandable):** A wrinkled-gold (wAu) capacitive stack -- dielectric, silicone elastomer, air gap, PDMS spacer -- sits over the artery. Each pulse compresses the micropillars and changes capacitance. An algorithm converts that signal to continuous systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial pressure.

**Headline:** Soft enough to wear. Accurate enough to trust.
