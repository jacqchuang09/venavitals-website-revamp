### The problem
**H2:** Blood pressure is continuous. Monitoring is not.

The standard cuff reads every 3 to 5 minutes. Between readings, a patient can lose significant blood volume or undergo a dangerous pressure drop that goes undetected until the next cycle. The alternative — arterial line cannulation — provides continuous, beat-to-beat data, but carries a 10–13% complication rate, a 0.6% infection risk, and a 5 to 20 minute placement time that delays the start of surgery. For cases where continuous pressure matters and an arterial line is not placed, clinicians are left choosing between incomplete data and invasive access.

**Stats:**
- 3–5 min — between cuff readings
- 10–13% — arterial line complication rate
- 0.6% — A-line infection risk
- 5–20 min — A-line placement time

**Pull quote:** "It's very binary... there's nothing really in between." — Anesthesiologist (inVibe survey, n=22)

---

### The solution
**H2:** Continuous. Non-invasive. Beat-to-beat.

VeriTrack is a soft, wearable sensor that adheres to the foot and measures blood pressure with every heartbeat — no cuff cycles, no needle. It delivers the continuous hemodynamic visibility of an arterial line without the procedural risk, placement delay, or invasive cannulation. Applied in pre-op and worn through the case, VeriTrack streams beat-to-beat arterial pressure to a bedside tablet, giving the care team a continuous signal from induction through emergence.

**Feature 1: Beat-to-beat resolution**
Every heartbeat, not every few minutes. Continuous BP trends without the lag of cuff inflation cycles — so pressure drops are visible as they happen, not after the next reading.

**Feature 2: Non-invasive placement**
No cannulation, no infection risk. Applied like a bandage and removed cleanly after the case. No arterial access, no line-care burden, no procedural delay.

**Feature 3: Wearable form factor**
Soft sensor, foot placement. Applied over the dorsalis pedis artery and kept entirely out of the surgical field. No occupied arm, no cuff interference, no tethered catheter.

**Regulatory note:** VeriTrack has been submitted for FDA 510(k) review and is not yet available for commercial sale.

## TECHNOLOGY

### Hero

---

### How it works
**H2:** How it works

The VeriTrack sensor sits over the dorsalis pedis artery on the foot. Its soft, stretchable material detects the subtle deflections of the artery beneath the skin with every heartbeat, converting that motion into a continuous blood pressure waveform — systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial pressure, beat to beat.

**Technical detail (expandable):** The sensing element is a wrinkled-gold (wAu) capacitive stack: a dielectric layer, silicone elastomer, air gap, and PDMS spacer. Each arterial pulse compresses the micropillar structure and changes the capacitance. An onboard algorithm converts those capacitance changes into continuous SBP, DBP, and MAP values with beat-to-beat resolution.

---

### The sensor
**H2:** Soft enough to wear. Accurate enough to trust.

The VeriTrack sensor is thin, flexible, and skin-conforming. It moves with the patient through position changes and motion without losing signal. Biocompatible materials mean no skin irritation over the course of a case. Applied in under five minutes in pre-op, it stays in place and monitors continuously — without the placement time, complication risk, or nursing burden of an arterial line.

**Callouts:** Skin-safe, biocompatible · Thin and conformal · No signal loss with motion

---

### The signal
**H2:** A continuous arterial waveform, not intermittent numbers.

Shown beside a simultaneous arterial-line trace, the VeriTrack waveform tracks each beat — including the systolic upstroke and dicrotic notch — across cases lasting up to five hours. Where the arterial line drops signal due to clotting or flushing artifacts, VeriTrack maintains a clean continuous trace.

In cases with rapid hemodynamic changes — acute hypotension, fluid loading, pressure swings exceeding 55 mmHg systolic range — VeriTrack tracked the arterial line beat for beat.

### Pain points
**H2:** The current choice is difficult.

**A-line delay:** Placing an arterial line can take 5 to 40 minutes, requires a skilled operator, and introduces procedural risk before the first incision. For cases that fall in the middle — where continuous pressure would be useful but a line is not clearly indicated — the choice between monitoring gaps and invasive access is a real clinical trade-off.

**A-line risk:** Arterial lines carry a 10–13% complication rate and a 0.6% infection risk per placement. In high-volume OR environments, that adds up across a year of cases.

**Cuff blind spots:** A cuff reads every 3 to 5 minutes with a 30 to 60 second lag. Sudden blood loss, fluid shifts, and rapid pressure changes can happen and resolve — or worsen — inside that window. The anesthesiologist sees the drop at the next reading, not when it starts.

---

### How VeriTrack fits
**H2:** Applied in pre-op. Continuous through the case.

VeriTrack is placed over the dorsalis pedis artery on the foot before the patient enters the OR. It streams beat-to-beat blood pressure to a bedside iOS device throughout the case. No arm access. No sterile field disruption. No interval re-application. After emergence, it peels off cleanly — no arterial line removal, no site care.

## SLEEP MEDICINE

**Notice banner:** Research use only. Not currently available for sleep medicine clinical use.

### Hero
**H1:** Beyond the AHI: See the cardiovascular impact of every apnea.
**Subhead:** VeriTrack is being studied as a research tool for continuous, noninvasive blood pressure monitoring during sleep.

---

### The opportunity
**H2:** The nocturnal BP problem

Sleep-disordered breathing is a major cardiovascular risk factor — but the standard AHI captures breathing events, not their hemodynamic impact. Every apnea triggers a blood pressure surge. In patients with OSA, those surges compound across hundreds of events per night, year after year, driving hypertension, cardiac remodeling, and stroke risk. Measuring them continuously has not been practical until now.

- 936 million people worldwide have sleep-disordered breathing
- 2–3x greater risk of heart attack or stroke
- 10 fewer years of life expectancy with untreated OSA
- 82–93% of patients remain undiagnosed

---

### What it captures
**H2:** Beat-to-beat through the night

VeriTrack records beat-to-beat blood pressure throughout the night, capturing the magnitude and frequency of BP spikes triggered by apneic events. In preliminary research, OSA subjects showed 51 surges per hour above 30 mmHg — with spikes exceeding a delta of 80 mmHg — compared to 0 surges per hour in healthy subjects. A CPAP split-night study showed clear reduction in BP variability with therapeutic pressure.
